## Datasets

- **listings_final_blocks**: the 56 blocks of the final live re-scan of all 8,883 Abu Dhabi listing sites
  (`data/places/rescan_final.db`), labelled by hand in `data/places/final_block_labels.csv`: 43 bad
  (gambling / harmful-redirect takeovers of business domains, young domains with data-theft or
  wallet-drainer scripts), 9 legitimate (mostly businesses listed on a phishing feed), 4 unlabelled. Labels
  are matched on a normalised URL (scheme, `www.`, case and trailing slash ignored) with the host as fallback.
- **places_blocks**: the 43 sites the engine blocked in the first 2,780 sites of the earlier scan, all
  listed businesses treated as legitimate (a few may really be compromised).
- **demo_scams**: the four blocked simulated scam sites of `docs/DEMO.md`, reviewed with `--no-site-fetch`.
- Fixtures in `tests/review/` cover young look-alikes, copied multi-page sites, credential theft, cloaking,
  scam reports, prompt injection, gambling takeovers and redirects to betting sites.

## How the review decides

1. Every block-carrying reason of the verdict becomes a claim and is tested against facts gathered
   independently, never repeated:
   - impersonation: does the site present itself as the brand (name or hostname), or only mention it; is the
     host the brand's own;
   - credential forms: where the data really goes (the site itself, a known form / payment / booking service,
     a popular provider, the site's own account on a provider);
   - **takeover** (`page.gambling`, `page.harmful_redirect`, `rule.takeover_block`, `page.unrelated_redirect`):
     the address is re-fetched; a redirect that still lands on a gambling / adult / pill site, gambling content
     on the page, or a recently registered name still forwarding to an unrelated site upholds the claim. The
     tests and word lists are the engine's own (`engine/rules.py`, `intel/data/lexicon.json`);
   - young domain, cloaking, blocklists.
2. An upheld claim confirms the block, whatever the history or identity of the domain: a takeover is about
   what the site does now, and the old history of a business name is evidence of a takeover, not against it.
3. A claim that could not be tested is not refuted: while any block-carrying claim is still open (including
   reason kinds the reviewer has no test for), the block stands (inconclusive). Only reasons listed as weak
   on their own in `policy.json` (`soft_reason_prefixes`: model scores, address features, scrambled
   scripts...) may stay untested.
4. When every accusation fell, positive evidence decides between allow and warn (thresholds in
   `src/scamshield/review/data/policy.json`): overturn needs 4 points with 3 from records the site cannot
   write (curated ownership, five years of history, or two years plus a top-sites rank); warn needs 2.5 with 1
   external; page evidence is capped at 2 points; history is capped at the current registration age; a
   blocklisted site is at most downgraded, and only with 5 points of which 3 external.
5. Wikidata and the DuckDuckGo panel prove ownership only with a second kind of source or an item with
   Wikipedia articles. An LLM judge sees the tested claims, may look further and writes the explanation; it
   can never be more lenient than the facts and cannot re-block a cleared site on its own word.
